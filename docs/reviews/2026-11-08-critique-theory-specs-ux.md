## 1. Bottom line

There is nothing built yet, so no feature is real or fake; what exists is four design documents. The most serious problem is that the documents present borrowed and invented numbers as established, and a clinician or science-fair judge would read them as evidence. The "100% sensitivity, 86% specificity" attributed to the Urian et al. paper comes from only 14 Alzheimer's patients and 14 matched controls, not from the thousands of people the spec names beside it; and the paper never mentions the atypical variants (PCA, lvPPA) the spec ties it to. Second, nothing in the plan collects the normative data, validation data, or base rates that the Z-score cutoffs and the "live syndrome probabilities" would need, so those displays would show invented numbers. Third, the plan sends patient voice and drawings to a hosted AI model with no consent, privacy, or intended-use section. The spec's own headline example (a high-functioning person who drops 1.6 standard deviations in a year) would not be flagged by its own reliable-change formula unless retest reliability is at least 0.667, and the source's closest retest figures are 0.49 to 0.71.
